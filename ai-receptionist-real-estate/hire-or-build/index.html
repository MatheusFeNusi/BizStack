AI receptionist: hire a service or build your own?

The verdict in one sentence: solo agents and teams up to 10 should hire a service, not build — because the hidden cost of building isn't the software, it's the ongoing maintenance of something that talks to your clients. This page has no affiliate links because the honest answer here isn't "buy our pick."

Who should hire a service

  • Anyone without a genuine interest in maintaining AI infrastructure as a side job.
  • Teams that need it working this month, not eventually.
  • Anyone whose CRM and calendar integrations matter more than perfect custom phrasing.

Who can justify building

  • Brokerages with in-house technical help (or an agency owner serving many agents).
  • Anyone with unusual call-flow requirements no vendor supports — genuinely unusual, not just preferred.
Who should skip both — for now: if you haven't set up missed-call text-back yet, do that first. It captures most of the value at a fraction of the cost, and it tells you whether your missed-call volume even justifies an AI receptionist.

The honest middle ground

Start with text-back this week. Track recovered conversations for a month. If real deal flow is coming through that channel, then evaluate done-for-you AI receptionist services with actual volume data — instead of buying on a demo.

FAQ

Is building cheaper?

On subscription cost, sometimes. On total cost including your hours and maintenance, rarely — for a small team.

What's the downside of hiring a service?

Monthly cost and less control over call flow and phrasing. You trade money for maintenance.

Where does an AI receptionist fit in my stack?

Layer three, on top of a working CRM and follow-up system. See the automation order of operations.
